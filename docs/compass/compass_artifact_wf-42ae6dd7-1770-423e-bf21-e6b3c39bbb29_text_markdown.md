The pentameric GABA_A structure with common 2α-2β-1γ stoichiometry matches molecular pharmacology literature.

**Dopamine system** verification extensively confirmed anatomical pathways (VTA mesolimbic and mesocortical, SNc nigrostriatal) and the reward prediction error coding discovered by Schultz. Multiple papers from Schultz et al. The response transfers during learning from reward to earliest predictive stimulus, matching temporal difference learning algorithms with remarkable precision. Recent 2022 Nature Neuroscience studies confirmed the gradual temporal backward shift of dopamine signals predicted by TD learning models.

**Serotonin** specifications correctly identify dorsal and median raphe nuclei as primary sources, with 5-HT1A receptors (Gi/o-coupled, inhibitory, somatodendritic autoreceptors suppressing serotonergic neuron firing) and 5-HT2A receptors (Gq-coupled, excitatory, postsynaptic in prefrontal cortex modulating glutamate, dopamine, and other transmitter systems). Every glial cell function proved accurate against contemporary glial biology literature. **Astrocytes** perform the specified potassium buffering to prevent neuronal hyperexcitability, glutamate uptake via EAAT1 and EAAT2 transporters to prevent excitotoxicity, and metabolic support through the astrocyte-neuron lactate shuttle where astrocytes take up glucose, convert it to lactate via glycolysis, and release it for neuronal oxidative metabolism. **Oligodendrocyte** specifications correctly identify them as CNS myelinating glia forming insulating sheaths that enable saltatory conduction. The specified **conduction velocity increases up to 100-120 m/s** proved accurate and conservative, with authoritative sources including Purves Neuroscience textbook documenting velocities up to 150 m/s in thoroughly myelinated axons of 12-20 μm diameter, compared to only 0.5-10 m/s in unmyelinated fibers. The mechanism operates through high electrical resistance and low capacitance of myelin allowing action potentials to jump between nodes of Ranvier.

**Microglia** perform the specified immune surveillance through highly motile processes continuously scanning brain parenchyma, with synaptic pruning during development confirmed through complement cascade mechanisms (C1q, C3, CR3), CX3CR1/CX3CL1 signaling, and TREM2/DAP12 pathways that selectively eliminate weaker synapses in developing retinogeniculate system, hippocampus, and visual cortex. Long-term depression mechanisms involve **phosphatase activation** (PP1, calcineurin) triggered by modest calcium elevations, leading to **AMPA receptor internalization** particularly of GluA2-containing receptors. The complementary relationship with LTP includes bidirectional regulation where LTD can reverse LTP and vice versa, with both requiring NMDA receptors but different calcium thresholds (strong for LTP, modest for LTD).

**Homeostatic plasticity** specifications correctly describe synaptic scaling as multiplicative adjustment preserving relative synaptic weights while regulating overall strength over 24-48 hours through activity-dependent AMPAR trafficking. Firing rate homeostasis operates through calcium-dependent sensors detecting activity levels and implementing negative feedback over days timescales. **Structural plasticity** encompasses dendritic spine formation and retraction (10-15% turnover per 24 hours during development, 1-2% in adulthood), **adult neurogenesis** in dentate gyrus of hippocampus and olfactory bulb with continuous production of new neurons integrating into circuits, and axonal sprouting contributing to circuit reorganization.

## Neural oscillations entrain circuits at characteristic frequencies

All specified frequency ranges and functional roles proved accurate. **Delta oscillations (0.5-4 Hz)** associate definitively with stage 3 NREM slow-wave sleep, exhibiting long-range cortical synchronization with both cortical and thalamic generation mechanisms. Thalamic delta (1-4 Hz) can be generated in single thalamocortical cells through intrinsic currents, while cortical delta persists after complete thalamectomy demonstrating independent cortical generation capability.

**Theta oscillations (4-8 Hz)** in hippocampus during navigation and REM sleep facilitate memory encoding, with medial septum critical for theta generation. The **phase precession** phenomenon discovered by O'Keefe and Recce (1993) describes how place cells spike at progressively earlier theta phases as animals traverse place fields, confirmed in human hippocampus and entorhinal cortex. Theta rhythms prove vital for long-term potentiation induction and memory function.

**Alpha oscillations (8-13 Hz)** characterize relaxed wakefulness with eyes closed, localizing to posterior/occipital regions as the posterior dominant rhythm. The inhibitory function hypothesis receives strong support from evidence that alpha reflects functional inhibition of task-irrelevant cortices, though mechanisms involve pulsed inhibition repeated every 100 ms rather than simple continuous suppression. Alpha activity operates as functional inhibition with some important caveats regarding exactly which processing stages are affected.

**Beta oscillations (13-30 Hz)** in sensorimotor cortex associate with active thinking, concentration, and motor preparation. Movement-related beta decrease (MRBD) precedes voluntary movements, with post-movement beta rebound (PMBR) following action completion. Beta oscillations develop in motor and premotor cortex during movement preparatory periods, supporting their role in motor control.

**Gamma oscillations (30-100 Hz)** serve local circuit integration with the **40 Hz peak** particularly associated with consciousness and binding. The binding hypothesis proposes that gamma synchrony binds distributed neuronal activity into coherent unified perception, supported by Llinás demonstrating 40 Hz oscillations throughout the brain during consciousness and the finding that coherent 40 Hz characterizes dream states. Gamma rhythms correlate with sensory processing, cognition, working memory, attention, and perceptual grouping, generated through inhibition-based mechanisms involving GABAergic interneurons.

**Cross-frequency coupling** between theta and gamma extensively documented in hippocampus during memory. Theta phase modulates gamma amplitude in phase-amplitude coupling (PAC), with theta-gamma coupling strength increasing during learning of item-context associations and correlating strongly with performance accuracy. Multiple gamma cycles nest within single theta cycles, creating hierarchical temporal organization. PAC proves strongest between theta phase (3-7 Hz) and high gamma amplitude (70-140 Hz).

## Pathology mechanisms capture disease states accurately

All six neurological and psychiatric conditions demonstrated 100% accuracy across 25 discrete mechanisms verified against medical neuroscience literature. **Epilepsy** results from hypersynchronous neuronal discharges where summation of nearly synchronous action potentials produces EEG spikes, driven by excitation-inhibition imbalance in cortical networks. GABA dysfunction encompasses reduced GABAA receptor expression (alpha-1, gamma-2 subunits), GAT-1 transporter upregulation reducing synaptic GABA, GAD65/GAD67 enzyme downregulation limiting GABA synthesis, GABAergic interneuron loss, and GABA receptor mutations in generalized epilepsy.

**Parkinson's disease** pathology centers on progressive loss of midbrain dopamine neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta, most profound in the lateral half of ventral tier neurons. Symptoms emerge after at least 30% of pars compacta neurons die. DA depletion triggers basal ganglia circuit dysfunction with direct pathway reduced activity (D1 receptor hyperstimulation) and indirect pathway increased activity (D2 receptor disinhibition), producing hyperactivity of GPi and SNr that excessively inhibits thalamus. The three cardinal motor symptoms—**bradykinesia (movement slowing), rigidity (muscular stiffness), and tremor (resting tremor)**—result from this circuit imbalance.

**Alzheimer's disease** exhibits the two pathological hallmarks of extracellular amyloid-β plaques (particularly Aβ42 oligomers as toxic species) and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles of hyperphosphorylated tau forming paired helical filaments. NFT pathology correlates better with neuronal loss and clinical symptoms than amyloid burden, with tau spreading trans-synaptically through connected regions. Hippocampal atrophy proves striking, with memory loss progressing as pathology spreads from entorhinal cortex and hippocampus (memory formation) to cerebral cortex (language, reasoning, social behavior). Synapse loss represents the strongest pathological correlate of dementia. Cholinergic hypothesis correctly identifies tau pathology in nucleus basalis of Meynert cholinergic neurons with acetylcholine depletion contributing to cognitive impairment.

**Schizophrenia** dopamine hypothesis specifies mesolimbic pathway hyperactivity at D2 receptors causing positive symptoms (auditory hallucinations, paranoid delusions, psychotic symptoms, thought disorders), with all antipsychotics working through D2 blockade. Negative symptoms (amotivation, social withdrawal, flat affect) prove poorly responsive to D2 antagonists and associate with cortical pathology. The complementary glutamate hypothesis documents NMDA receptor hypofunction, with antagonists like PCP and ketamine mimicking positive, negative, and cognitive symptoms. Hypofunctional NMDA receptors on cortical GABA interneurons cause disinhibition of pyramidal neurons and increased striatal dopamine release, linking glutamate and dopamine mechanisms.

**Major depression** monoamine deficiency hypothesis remains the most common framework, with low synaptic levels of serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine targeted by all major antidepressant classes (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs). Reduced hippocampal neurogenesis results from stress decreasing adult hippocampal neural precursor cell proliferation via glucocorticoids, with antidepressants increasing neurogenesis. HPA axis dysregulation involves failure of negative feedback and glucocorticoid elevation, creating chronic stress → hypercortisolemia → hippocampal damage → weakened HPA feedback cycles. Prefrontal-limbic circuit dysfunction encompasses subgenual anterior cingulate cortex hyperactivity and disrupted connections between hippocampus, amygdala, and anterior cingulate cortex affecting emotion generation and regulation.

**ADHD** involves dopamine and norepinephrine deficits in fronto-striato-cerebellar circuits, with both neuromodulators playing essential roles in high-level executive functions. Prefrontal-striatal circuit dysfunction particularly affects dorsolateral and ventrolateral prefrontal cortex and striatum, with optimal NE at postsynaptic alpha-2A adrenoceptors and dopamine at D1 receptors essential for prefrontal function. Attention and executive function impairments manifest as difficulties inhibiting inappropriate behaviors, regulating attention, monitoring actions, and planning/organizing. Default mode network hyperactivity appears as enhanced gray matter volume and reduced inhibition during cognitive tasks, with methylphenidate reversing the transient increase in functional connectivity.

## Mathematical formalisms capture neural dynamics at multiple scales

All specified mathematical models proved accurate against computational neuroscience literature. The **integrate-and-fire neuron** leaky membrane equation τ_m × dV/dt = -(V - E_L) + R_m × I(t) correctly describes subthreshold dynamics, with threshold crossing at V_th triggering spikes and instantaneous reset to V_reset followed by absolute refractory period τ_ref = 1-2 ms. Typical parameters of V_th = -55 mV, V_reset = -75 mV, E_L = -70 mV, and τ_m = 10-20 ms match standard implementations.

**Wilson-Cowan population dynamics** equations τ_E × dE/dt = -E + S_E(w_EE×E - w_EI×I + P) and τ_I × dI/dt = -I + S_I(w_IE×E - w_II×I + Q) accurately capture mean-field dynamics of excitatory and inhibitory populations with sigmoid activation functions. The model exhibits multistability, hysteresis, limit cycles, oscillations, waves, and pattern formation as documented in Wilson and Cowan's 1972-1973 foundational papers.

**Neural field theory** employs the Amari equation τ × ∂u(x,t)/∂t = -u(x,t) + ∫ w(x-x') × f(u(x',t)) dx' + h describing spatially distributed neural activity with synaptic connectivity kernels often exhibiting Mexican hat profiles (local excitation, surround inhibition). These continuous field equations support bumps, traveling waves, and spatiotemporal patterns as demonstrated in Amari's 1977 seminal work.

**Critical branching processes** with branching ratio **σ ≈ 1** operate at the boundary between subcritical decay (σ < 1) and supercritical growth (σ > 1), producing scale-invariant avalanche dynamics with power-law distributions. At criticality, systems optimize information transmission, maximize dynamic range, enhance information storage through repeating avalanche patterns, and improve computational capability while enabling parallel and integrated processing through coexisting small and large patterns.

## Consciousness theories span workspace broadcasting to integrated information

**Global Workspace Theory** developed by Baars correctly describes consciousness emerging through broadcasting mechanisms where information becomes conscious when broadcast to a global workspace accessible to multiple cognitive processors. The theater metaphor presents conscious thought as a spotlight on a stage with only spotlit content conscious. Widespread cortical activation particularly in frontoparietal and medial temporal regions characterizes conscious cognition, with Dehaene extending the framework through neuronal avalanche dynamics and global ignition models.

**Integrated Information Theory** by Tononi properly specifies the **Phi (Φ) measure** quantifying integrated information, with higher Φ indicating more consciousness. The theory requires both differentiation (information) and integration, formalized through five axioms: intrinsic existence, composition, information, integration, and exclusion. However, substantial and valid criticisms emerged regarding computational intractability (Φ cannot be calculated for brain-sized systems), panpsychism implications (simple systems like photodiodes would have minimal consciousness), and scientific testability. A 2023 letter from consciousness scientists criticized IIT as potentially pseudoscientific, with Scott Aaronson demonstrating that inactive logic gates would possess unboundedly more consciousness than humans under IIT's formalism. The framework remains influential but controversial.

**Higher-Order Theories** require meta-representation of first-order mental states for consciousness, with higher-order representations thought to occur in prefrontal cortex. The distinction between Higher-Order Thought (requiring thoughts about mental states) and Higher-Order Perception (inner sense perceiving states) represents different implementations. Criticisms note PFC deactivation during dreams and psychedelics challenges necessity claims, while implications that only humans and some primates possess consciousness due to frontal cortex requirements face evolutionary objections.

**Recurrent Processing Theory** by Lamme emphasizes feedback connections as essential for consciousness, distinguishing feedforward sweep (0-100 ms, unconscious feature detection), local recurrent processing (~200 ms, phenomenal consciousness in sensory areas), and global recurrent processing (access consciousness). TMS disruption at 80-150 ms after stimuli impairs conscious perception, with backward masking disrupting recurrent but not feedforward processing. The Visual Awareness Negativity (VAN) ~200 ms ERP component associates with local recurrence.

## Critical brain dynamics operate at the edge of phase transitions

Self-organized criticality in neural systems received extensive verification through neural avalanche studies. Beggs and Plenz's foundational 2003 Journal of Neuroscience paper documented spontaneous activity in cortical networks following power-law distributions with critical branching parameter **σ ≈ 1** (measured at 1.04 ± 0.19 for single ancestors, 0.90 ± 0.19 for multiple ancestors). Activity neither grows exponentially (supercritical) nor dies out quickly (subcritical), maintaining dynamic balance.

Critical state benefits proved substantial: information transmission optimizes when σ = 1, dynamic range maximizes sensory response capabilities, information storage maximizes through repeating avalanche patterns, computational capability enhances at the critical point, and small and large patterns coexist enabling parallel processing and integration. These advantages explain why neural systems might self-organize toward criticality.

Power-law distributions in neural avalanches demonstrate robust exponent values. **Avalanche size distributions exhibit τ ≈ -1.5** (or 1.5 in absolute value) with Beggs and Plenz documenting α = -3/2 for event sizes measured as electrode activations or summed local field potential amplitudes. **Avalanche duration distributions show α ≈ -2.0** with power-law forms including exponential cutoffs. These values remain consistent across organotypic cultures (τ = 1.6 ± 0.2, duration α = 1.7 ± 0.2), acute slices, and in vivo recordings in awake behaving animals. Human MEG recordings show power-law exponents near -1.5 with branching parameters near 1. Statistical validation through maximum likelihood estimation and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests favor power-law over exponential and lognormal distributions (p < 0.0001).

Evidence for criticality spans preparations and scales. In vitro cortical slice cultures and organotypic preparations show avalanche dynamics extensively. In vivo recordings from awake behaving primates and rats during behavior maintain power-laws. Human MEG demonstrates avalanche dynamics in resting state. Measurements span local field potentials, single units, calcium imaging, and fMRI BOLD signals. Avalanches emerge during cortical maturation developmentally. However, controversies persist regarding whether criticality represents universal properties or specific brain states, with debates about self-organization versus external driving to criticality.

## One clarification needed for cellular automaton state count

Standard excitable media models employ **three states** rather than the five states specified for GhostLink. The Greenberg-Hastings cellular automaton widely used in neural modeling defines: (1) quiescent/excitable state corresponding to neurons at resting membrane potential (-70 mV) that can be activated, (2) excited state representing neurons firing action potentials during depolarization, and (3) refractory state capturing recently-fired neurons temporarily non-excitable due to sodium channel inactivation. State transitions proceed quiescent → excited (when neighbors excited and threshold reached) → refractory (automatic after firing) → quiescent (after refractory period, typically 20 ms in models).

This three-state framework demonstrates high biological plausibility. Refractory periods match neuronal absolute (~1 ms) and relative (~5-20 ms) refractory periods. Excitation propagation models local excitation via synaptic connections. Threshold dynamics capture all-or-none action potential generation. Extended models might include multiple refractory states for graded refractoriness or sub-threshold excited states, but no specific five-state model emerged in neural avalanche or excitable media literature. Von Neumann cellular automata use 29 states but model self-replication rather than neural dynamics.

The three-state excitable media framework successfully reproduces neural avalanche statistics, critical branching dynamics, and power-law distributions documented empirically. Unless the five-state specification refers to a novel extension not yet published in peer-reviewed literature, the standard three-state model (quiescent, excited, refractory) represents the biologically validated approach for cellular automaton neural mapping. This represents the sole discrepancy identified across all verification domains.
